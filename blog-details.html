How to Cut & Install Large-Format Porcelain Tile with a 10" Wet Saw

Jack Callahan · September 2026 · 8 Min Read

Large-format tiles—typically defined as anything with one side greater than 15 inches—bring a sleek, seamless elegance to modern walk-in showers and kitchen floors. However, their dense vitreous composition makes them prone to jagged chips or sudden stress fractures when cut with standard manual snap cutters.

1. Adjusting Water Delivery & Blade Alignment

A continuous stream of water is critical. It does more than suppress silica dust: it cools the diamond matrix rim of the blade, preventing friction-induced thermal shock that causes tile cracking. Ensure the dual water nozzles point directly at both the front and rear of the blade contact point.

2. The "Back-Score" Technique for Zero Chipping

When making a straight rip cut across a 24-inch porcelain slab, do not push through the final edge immediately. Instead, back-score the rear 1/2 inch of the tile first, pull the tile back, and then cut through from the front. This eliminates corner blowout as the blade exits.
